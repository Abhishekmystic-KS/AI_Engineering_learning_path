RAG EMBEDDINGS

QUESTION
What are embeddings in RAG?

ANSWER
An embedding is a list of numbers that represents the meaning of text. Similar pieces of text are placed close together in a mathematical vector space, while unrelated pieces are placed farther apart.

For example, these sentences should have similar embeddings:

- How do I reset my password?
- I forgot my password. How can I change it?

The words are different, but the meaning is similar.

HOW EMBEDDINGS WORK IN RAG

1. A document chunk is sent to an embedding model.
2. The model converts the chunk into a vector.
3. The vector and original text are stored together.
4. The user's question is converted into another vector.
5. The system compares the question vector with document vectors.
6. The closest vectors are returned as relevant information.

VECTOR EXAMPLE

  Text: "The office closes at 6 PM."
  Embedding: [0.12, -0.44, 0.81, 0.33, ...]

The numbers are not meant to be read by humans. They are used for mathematical similarity searches.

SIMILARITY SEARCH

RAG systems commonly compare vectors using:

- Cosine similarity
- Dot product
- Euclidean distance

The exact method depends on the embedding model and vector database.

EMBEDDING MODELS

Embedding models can be hosted services or open-source models. Examples include models from OpenAI, Cohere, Hugging Face, and Sentence Transformers.

IMPORTANT PRACTICES

- Use the same embedding space for documents and user questions.
- Choose a model that supports the language and domain of your data.
- Do not change the embedding model without re-indexing existing documents.
- Test retrieval quality with real user questions.
- Store the original text next to each vector so it can be sent to the LLM.

LIMITATION

Embeddings represent meaning, but they do not understand truth. A wrong or outdated document can still receive a strong similarity score. Retrieval results must be checked and evaluated.
